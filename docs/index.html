            Reading these numbers

            Expectancy is the average net return per closed trade. It is the single most useful number here: positive expectancy with enough trades means the edge is real, and win rate alone tells you very little.

            Profit factor divides the sum of winning returns by the sum of losing returns. Above 1.0 is profitable; above 1.5 is healthy for a trend-following system.

            Max drawdown is the deepest peak-to-trough fall of the equity curve above, not the worst single trade.

            Position sizing matters to the headline. The equity curves assume each trade takes 20% of capital, five slots in parallel. Sizing every trade at full capital would compound the same trades into a far larger number, which would be meaningless because signals overlap in time. Per-trade figures like win rate, expectancy and payoff ratio are unaffected by sizing.

            Trailing-stop systems win less than half the time. Small losses are cut quickly and a few large winners carry the result, so a win rate near 40% alongside a payoff ratio above 2 is the expected shape, not a warning sign.

            Caveats. The live ledger begins when trade logging was added, so early history is thin. Entry price uses the signal day's close as a proxy for the next open, which understates slippage on gap-up entries. The backtest uses today's watchlist, so it carries survivorship bias and should be read as a sanity check on the logic, not a forecast. Nothing here is investment advice.
